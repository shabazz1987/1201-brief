Lede

The one sentence that names the day. Not a quote. Not a decision list. The brief speaking.

Proposedbrief paragraph · 17px

Ship the sentence as a brief paragraph. Body size 17. No pull bar, no chips, no split. One block, then the day can proceed.

Lindsee is in the studio this afternoon. Two things still need a yes before Friday print.

Thursday, August 20, 2026 · Los Angeles

The afternoon is 1:00–3:00pm PT. Briefing, not her start. The two yeses are Stone Island brochure_12 and RA Version 2. The lede names them without listing them.

Variation Ablockquote.pull

Same sentence, structurally a pull. Left rule. Use when the lede should feel excerpted from a longer brief, not written as the page.

Lindsee is in the studio this afternoon. Two things still need a yes before Friday print.

This is brief voice. Do not attribute it to Lindsee, Nyshka, or anyone in the room. A pull is not speech.

Variation Bquote + chips

The sentence stays. The jobs it points at become chips. Lindsee, Stone Island, RA, print Friday. The chips are the map; the sentence is still the lede.

Lindsee is in the studio this afternoon. Two things still need a yes before Friday print.
Lindsee Stone Island RA print Friday
Stone Island RA print Friday

Chips are softer than badges. Badges are the board. Either can sit under a lede. Do not turn the sentence itself into badges.

Variation Ctwo short sentences, split

The locked sentence is already two. Split them. First the studio. Then the two yeses. Stacked, not one paragraph.

Lindsee is in the studio this afternoon.

1:00–3:00pm PT · briefing · guidebook locked

Two things still need a yes before Friday print.

brochure_12 · Nyshka Version 2

Splitting is a structure change. It makes the two yeses a second beat. Proposed keeps them in one breath because that is how the day was named.
